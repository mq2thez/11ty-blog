Hello! The world is moving at a wild pace these days, and it's possible that recent news has caused you to want to ~~flee~~ move to Ireland (or somewhere else in Europe). Ireland has a wonderful "Critical Skills Visa" for tech workers, so it may seem like a great place to ~~escape to~~ use as a home base for exploring the rest of Europe.

My wife and I decided to move to Ireland from the USA in October 2020, arrived in September 2021, and moved back to the US in August 2024. A lot of people have asked me about the experience in the last few days. I'd like to talk a bit about the things we found out (generally the hard way) and why we ended up moving back to the US, in order to hopefully help other folks know what they might be getting in to.

For people who don't want to read a long post filled with detail, a TLDR: consult an Irish accountant who works with expats and an immigration lawyer to get a **very clear** understanding of what will happen when you try to do this. 



* Your income will be significantly lower
* Your taxes will be significantly higher and significantly more complex
* Cost of living is essentially the same
* Mortgages

# Visa and Immigration

In order to get a "Critical Skills" visa for Ireland, you need to fit into a narrow grouping of careers. Assuming you do fit into that grouping, you will need to be sponsored by a company who has specific role openings for foreign workers. That is, you won't be able to just go get any job -- you will need to get a job with a larger company who works with the Irish government in order to sponsor people who come in. Even if your current company has an Irish office, they may not be set up to sponsor people or may not have any available sponsorships to give. Don't expect that your current company will be able to sponsor you.

Once you _do_ get a sponsored job, getting your Visa itself can be a righteous pain the ass. In 2021, it took **months** for me to get approved to move to the country due to the backlog of Critical Skills Visa requests that the Irish government couldn't keep up with. I moved while staying with my employer at the time, which thankfully simplified a lot of things -- I kept working for them in the US until the time came to move over, then I moved to Ireland while taking PTO and started working on Irish time. I won't go into the details (since they're specific to my company and not relevant to others), but I will say that my company handled this process so poorly that if I'd known ahead of time what would have been involved, I would have just quit and found a new job that was willing to sponsor me. All said and done, it took about 9 months from when my company first approved my move until we actually landed in Ireland.

Once we got to Ireland, there was a long process to actually get my Visa approved and mark that I had permission to be in the country. You have 90 days to complete this process and have your new Visa card in order to re-enter the country. It took more than a month to actually get the appointment with Irish immigration in order to submit all of my information, and once I got there and submitted everything, my actual Visa card never arrived. We waited weeks, called Immigration every day, etc -- nothing. We weren't able to safely leave the country or do any traveling during this time because of the fear that I would get sent back at the border. We had to get help from volunteer immigration groups in order to finally get the matter resolved, who found out that a printer had malfunctioned on the day that Immigration had been printing out my card, and they had simply _never reprinted my card to be sent out_. It still took weeks and multiple interventions by the volunteer group in order for me to get the card. It ended up arriving the same day as a major family emergency that required my wife to fly back to the States without me (in part because we didn't have the card when we needed to plan).

Once you've gotten your Visa card, great! It lasts for two years. You **have** to work for that company and at that job for one year. You have significantly improved employee protections during that time period, and your employer will have to jump through a lot of legal hoops to even lay you off as part of a mass layoff. But you can't quit or change jobs. After a year, another company can theoretically "buy out" your visa for the second year of the visa. That visa can't be renewed -- instead, after 20 months, you need to apply for a different type of visa. This process can take months, and _also_ runs the risk of Irish Immigration taking way too long, but at the end of it, your new visa lets you essentially work for any company in Ireland and stay indefinitely (though you have to renew it every two years) as long as you aren't unemployed for longer than 6 months at a time.

A lot of people know that you can get Irish citizenship if you stay for five years after arriving on a Critical Skills visa. What we didn't know until we were already there is that in order to qualify for this, there are maximum numbers of days you can be out of Ireland during each year (among other qualificiation criteria). In the year before you apply for cititzenship, for example, you can only spend 70 days outside of Ireland. This seems like a lot of days until you start thinking about trips back to the US for work, etc. We also heard from lots of people that the actual application process took a _very_ long time, so it's not like you could just assume that 5 years in you would be getting a passport. Some folks were waiting 7-8 years.
